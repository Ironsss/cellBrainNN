    What this is (and is not)

    NeuroDish is a computational simulation inspired by neurons-in-a-dish experiments. The agent is a tabular Q-learning algorithm. The network drawn in the Neural activity panel is an activity representation of that agent; it does not learn and it is not a measurement of biological tissue.

    State representation (486 discrete states)
    • Approach: ball moving toward / away from the paddle (2)
    • Intercept offset: predicted ball y at the paddle plane minus paddle y, in 9 signed bins (≤12, ≤35, ≤80, ≤160, >160 px) (9)
    • Time to contact: <0.5 s, 0.5–1.2 s, >1.2 s (3)
    • Paddle zone and ball vertical zone: top / middle / bottom (3 × 3)

    Actions: Up, Stay, Down.

    Reward function
    • Interception: + reward magnitude. Miss: − penalty magnitude (terminal).
    • Shaping (Reinforced only, optional): ±0.02 × normalized reduction of distance to the predicted intercept, and −0.002 for moving when already aligned. Small by design so it cannot outweigh the task reward.
    • Outcome-only: the same task reward without shaping (sparse).
    • No reinforcement: the policy runs but no value updates occur. Previously learned behavior is not erased.

    The update is Q(s,a) ← Q(s,a) + α[r + γ·maxₐ′ Q(s′,a′) − Q(s,a)], with the future term set to 0 on a miss. Episodes truncated by the rally cap still bootstrap.

    Metric definitions
    • Episode: one rally; it ends at a miss or after the rally cap of returns.
    • Interceptions / misses: ball arrivals at the paddle plane that were hit / missed.
    • Interception rate: interceptions ÷ (interceptions + misses), shown as — until an attempt exists.
    • Recent rate: same ratio over the last N episodes (rolling window).
    • Cumulative reward: sum of task rewards (excluding shaping) in the selected dataset.
    • Exploration ε: probability of a random action. Decays per training episode.
    • States visited / updates: distinct states and total Q-updates applied.

    Training, evaluation and manual datasets are tracked separately.

    Audio is feedback, not an input

    The success tone and the noise burst are rendered for the observer. They are not fed to the agent, so muting them does not change learning. Reinforcement conditions change the numeric learning signal only.

    Evidence of learning

    A rising training curve alone is not proof: it mixes exploration with learning. Run a frozen-policy evaluation and a random-policy baseline on the same seed, then compare intervals in the Experiment sheet.

    Neural activity

    Activity representation of the Q-learning agent. It does not learn.

    Excitatory flow Inhibitory flow Reward event Penalty / negative TD

    Inputs and hidden units are a fixed, deterministic encoding of the observation. Output nodes show the agent's real Q-values; the ring marks the selected action.

    Reseeding restarts the environment and exploration streams; the learned model is kept.

    Initial ε applies on reset. Changes to α, γ, decay and minimum apply immediately. Lower decay values explore less, sooner.

    Audio is rendered for the observer only. It is not an input to the agent. Sounds are skipped above 4× speed.

    Clears the Q-table, training history and evaluations. Saved models in the browser are untouched.

    Evaluation freezes the policy (no learning, no exploration) and runs fixed-seed episodes. Training metrics are not affected.

    Seed comparison

    Trains two fresh agents headlessly with different seeds under the current condition and parameters, evaluating each periodically against a random baseline.

    NEURODISH

    An artificial neural learning experiment

    1. Observe an untrained agent trying to intercept a ball.
    2. Start training and let reinforcement shape its policy.
    3. Watch activity signals and performance change.
    4. Evaluate the frozen policy against a random baseline.
    5. Compare conditions, seeds and reward settings.

    This is a computational simulation inspired by neuroscience. The nodes and synapse-like lines are a visualization of an artificial agent's computations, not measurements of biological neurons.
